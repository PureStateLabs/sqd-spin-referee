**Cast:** Claude (Anthropic) executing autonomously; a human director setting goals and gates; no other personnel.
**Span:** 2026-07-08 → 2026-07-15 (~7.5 days of campaign time).
**Question:** Do quantum-circuit samples beat classical selected-CI at matched cost (the live dispute between IBM's flagship SQD results, arXiv:2405.05068 / Sci. Adv. 2025, and the Reinholdt-group critique, arXiv:2501.07231)?

Day 14's answer had been that the primary source carries no penalty, fact-checked twice. **Both fact-checks were wrong.** Grepping the raw arXiv HTML bytes — v1, v2, v3, plus ar5iv and the published version, no extraction layer in the loop — finds in every version since May 2024: total spin conserved "by a soft constraint in the eigenstate solver … a penalty term to mitigate spin contamination," (H + λ[S² − s(s+1)]²)|ψ⟩ = E|ψ⟩, employed at λ = 0.2. The Day-14 verification had trusted web-extraction reads that silently false-negatived on the same document — the exact failure mode this paper indicts (accepting an instrument's not-found without checking the instrument), committed by us, twice, and settled only because the author pressed for source-level certainty on a load-bearing claim. Three paper statements were retracted and reframed (§1's "penalty off entirely," §3's "sole mechanism" sentence, Table 11's flagship row), a penalty-provenance limitation was added, `_sqdpen.py`'s header comment was corrected, and the reviewer's allegation is now credited as confirmed.

The scientific response, as on Day 14, was to run it. `_sqdpen_sq.py` solves the flagship's exact stated operator — the squared H + 0.2·[S²]², which PySCF's `fix_spin_` does not implement (it is linear-only, so the stated form requires a dedicated matrix–vector operator) — on the captured benchmark-energy subspace, λ = 0 anchor certified against the shipped record, two independent solves agreeing bit-for-bit before a warm-started polish. Result: **+528 mHa at ⟨S²⟩ = 3.010** — the stated form at the stated strength pays 11.6 times the unpenalized error and lands three units of spin from the singlet; the +2.9 Ha singlet floor is form-independent (both penalties share the exact-singlet null space). And the squared form sharpened the mechanism into its cleanest exhibit: the converged state is nearly an eigenvector of the *projected* PS²P (in-subspace variance 0.009) concentrated at 3.01, which is not S(S+1) for any S, while its first moment alone algebraically forces physical Var(S²) ≥ 3.0. The flagship's own penalty, at its own strength, produces sharpness in the wrong operator. Gates: audit grew to 363/0 (section X2 re-derives the squared point from `sqdpen_sq_it4.npz`, positive gates pin the corrected framing in both sources, negative gates ban the retracted claims from ever returning); the manuscript-wide em-dash/cadence pass landed in the same freeze, and the self-audit narrative (the paper's former appendix) moved out of the manuscript into this trail, now its sole home — the paper keeps only the formal AI-use disclosure. ### Day 18 — 2026-07-20 (the squared penalty's operator is challenged, rebuilt, and re-run — v5.9.1)

Review round 12, the strongest of the twelve (it calls the central spin-identity result "very strong" and the flagship-scale reconstruction "the hardest result to dismiss"), raised one real technical objection: the v5.9 squared-penalty run (`_sqdpen_sq.py`) forms the penalty by applying `contract_ss` twice, i.e. the *projected* square (PS²P)², whereas the flagship's stated operator (S²)², compressed onto the fixed determinant subspace P, is the *literal* square P(S²)²P. The two differ by PS²(I − P)S²P ⪰ 0, the leakage of S² through the subspace complement, which is precisely the object this paper's central result says is nonzero ([PHP, PS²P] ≠ 0). Calling the projected-square number "the flagship's exact stated operator" was therefore an over-claim, the same class as the Day-17 penalty-provenance slip (a load-bearing operator identity taken on trust), caught one layer deeper and, again, by review.

The response, as before, was to build it and run it. `_sqdpen_true.py` constructs the literal compression P(S²)²P from the exact M_s = 0 spin ladder (the same S⁺ machinery as the second-moment kernel: sparse raising operators R1 mapping P to M_s+1 and R2 to M_s+2, so that P(S²)²P c = R1ᵀ(R2ᵀ S⁺²c + 2 S⁺c)), and a three-way gauge (G0) validates it against PySCF's independent S² to machine precision: the ladder reproduces `contract_ss` exactly (0.0), the literal-square matvec matches a dense projected S⁴ exactly (0.0), and it differs from the projected square by up to 2.0 (the leakage is real, and the test discriminates). At the stated λ = 0.2 the two compressions bracket the outcome, and neither is a competitive singlet. The projected square stays pinned at ⟨S²⟩ = 3.010 / +528 mHa (a near-eigenvector of PS²P at an unphysical spin value, the mechanism's cleanest exhibit, retained). The literal square, being the stronger penalty (it registers the unpenalized state's full ⟨S⁴⟩ ≈ 47, not its in-subspace proxy ≈ 23), drives the ground past a sharp threshold to the singlet floor: ⟨S²⟩ = 0.004 at +2,884 mHa on the D = 441² operating point (⟨S²⟩ = 0.000 at +3,034 mHa on D = 245²), the +2.9 Ha floor itself. The endpoint coincidence is guaranteed, not fitted: (S²)², (PS²P)², and the linear S² are all positive semidefinite with the identical null space, so every penalty of this family shares the same floor in any compression. The reviewer's objection thus resolves into a strengthening: the flagship's own stated operator, run literally at its stated strength, reaches a singlet only 2.9 Ha too high; run as a subspace-confined Krylov solve, it never leaves the contaminated band. Either way, no competitive singlet.

Gates: the audit X2 section now certifies both compressions (the projected square from `sqdpen_sq_it4.npz`; the literal square from `sqdpen_true_it4.npz`, gauged and λ = 0-anchored), with positive gates pinning the two-compression framing and the leakage operator in both sources and a negative gate retiring the "solved exactly that operator" over-claim; the count grows to 368/0. This is the ninth self-caught-or-review-prompted detection and the second reviewer-prompted correction; the formal disclosure and Table 11 are updated accordingly. Reissued as v5.9.1.

### Day 19 — 2026-09-11 (a published clause fails a check against the data owner's own loader — v5.9.7)

While drafting a question for the owner of IBM's data archive about the 58.6M-configuration file, the agent checked one of its own published clauses, that the file contains no Hartree–Fock string, against the loader the archive ships. The loader prints row 0 under the label "Hartree Fock string:". The clause was wrong: the string is present exactly once, at row 0. The error had a mechanical origin. `_lucjopt.py` logs the HF weight to four decimal places, 1/58,644,960 prints as 0.0000, and that was read as absence. It survived every audit because it was never pinned: `_paperaudit.py` verified the entry count and the in-sector rate of that file, not this clause.

It was caught before the question was sent, so the archive's owner never received it. It had, however, shipped in v5.9.5, the public release, and it was still in v5.9.6, which was never published. The response was the usual one, re-derive before correcting. `_hfcheck.py` settles it from IBM's raw files alone, three ways: the energy of row 0's determinant under their FCIDUMP equals their tabulated RHF energy to 1×10⁻⁷ with no assumption about bit order; it is the row their loader labels Hartree–Fock; and a fresh full scan finds that row once, at index 0. v5.9.7 corrects the clause, drops the unverified gloss that came with it, and adds two checks (the count from the data, the wording from the manuscript) that make the audit fail on the old text. 370/0.

The lesson is narrow and worth keeping. A number printed at fixed precision that rounds to zero is not evidence of absence, and a claim about someone else's file should be checked against their own tooling before it is published, or sent to them.

### Why the self-caught defects are the point

Six of the first ten detections came from **pre-registered internal gates and cross-checks** (known-answer spin labels, mapping round-trips, machine verification of the manuscript against the archives, agreement with the subject's own published tables), not from external review — and the biggest casualty of the audit was our own most-wanted result. The seventh (Day 15) was prompted by an external reviewer's request for a deeper measurement and caught by independent re-measurement of our own stored record — the same discipline, triggered from outside. External review has since added corrections of its own — a wrong justification for a correct classification in one table, a quotable-surface language pass, and (after the v5.5 freeze) one abstract-level claim-boundary vulnerability, then a two-line claim-precision pass on the re-review; no number touched in any of them — all folded into these same gates. The eighth detection (Day 17) is the one where the audit itself was the failing instrument: round 14 alleged that the flagship used an active λ = 0.2 spin penalty, our source check twice false-negatived the penalty and we wrongly declared it absent, and a later round re-verified from the raw arXiv source, confirmed the reviewer, and retracted our characterization on the record. The ninth (Day 18) is a second reviewer-prompted correction of the same protocol, one level deeper: the squared penalty we had run was the projected square (PS²P)², not the literal P(S²)²P the flagship's equation denotes, so we built the literal operator, gauged it against PySCF to machine precision, and ran it. We answered the right way each time by running: the shipped linear penalty (Day 14, v5.6), the flagship's stated squared form as the projected square (Day 17, v5.9), and its literal compression (Day 18, v5.9.1) all trace the same spin–energy frontier to the same +2.9 Ha singlet floor, in every form and compression. The tenth (Day 19) is the first caught after publication: a clause saying IBM's 58.6M-configuration file holds no Hartree–Fock string, read off a weight that prints as 0.0000 at four decimals, never pinned, and wrong, since the string is there once, at row 0. It was caught by checking the claim against the data owner's own loader before sending them a question about it, and v5.9.7 corrects it with the checks that would have stopped it. The eleventh (Day 19, v6.0) never left the working copy: rewriting the prose exposed a misstatement in the unsubmitted journal compression, which had described one released-pipeline run as using IBM's samples when it used samples drawn from our own converged vector. Both failures and their catches are in this trail because that is the point: the gate discipline applies to us most of all. That is the property any AI-assisted computational science should be required to demonstrate. We're publishing the gates along with the findings.
